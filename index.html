Slow Sky

Start with seven stars.

Orion is the easiest constellation to find on autumn and winter evenings. Learn it once, and the rest of the sky starts to make sense.

What’s overhead in November

Early to mid-month

Taurid meteors. There are only a few an hour, but they tend to be slow and bright, which makes them easy to follow with your eyes.

Around November 17–18

The Leonids peak. It’s a modest shower, roughly ten to fifteen meteors an hour at best under dark skies. A bright moon will wash out the fainter ones, so check the moon phase first.

All month, evenings

Orion clears the eastern horizon in the evening and stands high in the south by the early hours. Find the belt, then follow it down and to the left to reach the brightest star in the night sky, Sirius.

Shower dates shift by a day or two each year. Check an almanac for your location.

Slow Sky is a small guide for people who have never done this before. Go outside, wait, look up.
